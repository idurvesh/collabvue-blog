<h2 class="blog-h2">The four kinds of &quot;platform&quot; you are actually comparing</h2>
<p class="blog-p">When people say &quot;influencer marketing platform in India,&quot; they usually mean one of these four. They look similar from a landing page and behave nothing alike once you are inside.</p>
<h3 class="blog-h3">Managed and enterprise platforms</h3>
<p class="blog-p">These are the full-service ones. You brief them, they plan the campaign, source the creators, handle contracts and content approvals, and hand you a report at the end. They are really an agency wearing a software coat, and they are built for brands spending serious money across many campaigns a year.</p>
<p class="blog-p">What to expect: monthly retainers or per-campaign fees, sometimes a percentage of your total creator spend on top, and a minimum budget that quietly rules out small brands. If you are a national brand with a marketing team and no time to manage creators yourself, this is a real option. If you run one café or a young D2C label, you are paying for a layer of people to do something you could do in an afternoon.</p>
<h3 class="blog-h3">Discovery and analytics tools</h3>
<p class="blog-p">These are search engines for creators. You get filters, audience data, fake-follower checks, and lists you can export. What they usually do not do is the outreach, the negotiation, or the payment. Those stay your job.</p>
<p class="blog-p">They are genuinely useful if you are running influencer marketing at volume and need to vet dozens of accounts a week on data. For a brand doing two or three collabs, you are paying a subscription for a database when the harder part, actually getting a reply and agreeing a rate, is still entirely on you.</p>
<h3 class="blog-h3">Self-serve marketplaces and directories</h3>
<p class="blog-p">Here you browse creators yourself, see their rates and past work, and contact them directly. No account manager, no managed campaign. You are in charge, and you pay the creator directly. This is the lightest, cheapest layer, and it is where a small brand running local campaigns almost always belongs. CollabVue is this type.</p>
<p class="blog-p">The trade-off is real and worth saying out loud: you do the work. You pick the creators, write the brief, and run the collab. What you save is the managed-service fee and, on the honest ones, the commission.</p>
<h3 class="blog-h3">Creator networks and talent rosters</h3>
<p class="blog-p">Some platforms are really the front door to a fixed roster of creators they manage or represent, a bit like a talent agency&#x27;s list. You get consistency and vetting, but you are limited to their people, and their commission is usually baked into whatever you pay.</p>
<p class="blog-p">These suit brands who want a curated shortlist and do not mind paying for the curation. Just know you are seeing that network&#x27;s creators, not the open market, so the &quot;best fit for your budget&quot; may simply not be on the list.</p>
<h2 class="blog-h2">How to compare any platform on the four things that matter</h2>
<p class="blog-p">Ignore the homepage adjectives. Judge every platform in India on these four, and the right choice for your situation becomes obvious fast.</p>
<p class="blog-p"><strong class="blog-strong">How it charges you.</strong> There are only a few real models: a subscription you pay whether you run a campaign or not, a commission taken as a cut of your creator spend, a managed fee per campaign, or a pay-only-when-you-use-it credit like CollabVue&#x27;s connects. A subscription punishes you for a slow month. A commission means the platform earns more when you spend more on creators, which is not always your interest. Work out what you actually pay in a normal month before you commit.</p>
<p class="blog-p"><strong class="blog-strong">Where its creators are, and how local.</strong> A platform with a huge national roster is not automatically better for you. If you run a Mumbai studio or a Bangalore café, you need creators whose audience is in your city, not a database of a hundred thousand accounts spread across the country. Ask how it verifies creators and whether you can filter down to your city and suburb, because for any business people physically visit, locality beats raw reach every time.</p>
<p class="blog-p"><strong class="blog-strong">Whether you talk to creators directly.</strong> Some platforms sit between you and the creator forever, so every message and every payment routes through them and their cut. Others hand you the contact and step out of the way. Direct contact is cheaper and faster, and it means the rate you agree is the rate the creator receives. A managed layer is worth paying for only when you genuinely do not have time to run the collab yourself.</p>
<p class="blog-p"><strong class="blog-strong">The real minimum to get started.</strong> Many enterprise platforms have a floor, whether they advertise it or not, below which they are not interested. If your first campaign is worth twenty or thirty thousand rupees total, a platform built for lakhs-per-month brands will either reject you or overcharge you. Match the tool to the size of the job.</p>
<h2 class="blog-h2">The question most of these guides skip: do you even need a platform?</h2>
<p class="blog-p">Here is the thing a platform&#x27;s own website will never tell you. For a small brand running local micro and nano campaigns, you do not need a heavy managed platform at all. You need a way to find the right local creators, a clear brief, and a direct line to message them. That is a directory, not an agency, and it is the cheapest layer there is.</p>
<p class="blog-p">The expensive, full-service platforms earn their fee on scale and complexity: many campaigns, big budgets, legal and reporting overhead, a marketing team with no time. If that is not you yet, paying for it is money that should have gone to the creators. I made the same argument in more detail about agencies specifically in <a class="blog-link" href="/blog/influencer-marketing-agency-mumbai">looking for an influencer marketing agency in Mumbai</a>, and the logic is identical here: the middle layer is worth it sometimes, and quietly overpriced for a small brand most of the time.</p>
<p class="blog-p">So before you compare platforms feature by feature, be honest about your own size. A first campaign with three local creators does not need enterprise software. It needs the light version.</p>
